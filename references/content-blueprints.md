# Content blueprints

Use these blueprints after extracting the thesis and before writing a JSON spec.

## Research or trend analysis: 8–10 slides

1. **Promise** — name the tension and tell the reader what they will understand.
2. **Scale** — show the strongest verified indicators of relevance.
3. **Distinction** — compare commonly confused concepts.
4. **Mechanism** — explain how the thing actually works.
5. **Value** — quantify money, time, risk, adoption, or impact.
6. **Debate** — present the strongest objection or limitation fairly.
7. **Judgment** — state the author's decision rule or timing threshold.
8. **Deeper layer** — connect to a framework, system, or local context.
9. **Conclusion** — compress the insight and give segmented next actions.

## Practical guide: 6–8 slides

1. outcome and intended reader;
2. prerequisites or diagnosis;
3. first action;
4. second action;
5. common failure and correction;
6. checklist or decision tree;
7. example result;
8. concise next step.

## Comparison: 6–7 slides

1. comparison question and recommendation preview;
2. evaluation dimensions;
3–5. evidence by dimension;
6. who should choose each option;
7. final recommendation and caveat.

## Existing-deck optimization

For each supplied slide, record:

- current purpose;
- whether that purpose is unique in the sequence;
- strongest element to preserve;
- hierarchy or density problem;
- proposed block type;
- keep, merge, split, reorder, or delete decision.

Then rebuild the sequence. Do not polish slides that should be removed.

## Evidence rules

- Separate sourced facts from author interpretation.
- Put a short source next to the relevant number or quote.
- If a claim cannot be verified, label it as an opinion, hypothesis, or user-provided statement.
- Prefer fewer strong facts over many weak ones.
- Never create a visual citation that implies verification did not occur.
